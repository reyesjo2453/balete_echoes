## The pitch

> **The Dimaculangans have run a funeral parlor in Quiapo for four generations. They bury the dead by day.
> By night, they walk them home.**

The family are **sundô**. In Filipino folk belief the *sundô* is the one who fetches the dying. For generations the
family has guided restless souls to the ferrywoman **Magwayen** before something hungrier finds them first. Their only
tool is Lolo Pido's pocket watch, cut from the heartwood of the balete where the death god **Sidapa** marks how long
every person will live.

The watch can **borrow time**: undo a blow, hold back a flood, keep a soul from slipping away. Every borrowed second
is written against the family's own lifespans. That is the real *utang*, and it is coming due.

**Theme:** *Everything is borrowed, and every debt gets collected.* The money debt on the parlor, *utang na loob*,
the family's borrowed time, the old pact with the spirit courts, and Bakunawa coming to collect what the world owes
the moon are all the same idea at different sizes.

**Inspirations:** *Supernatural* (the family business, a monster-of-the-week case each episode, a season arc, an
estranged sibling, the gruff ally who is basically Bobby), *Bleach* (an ordinary young man who sees the dead, a hidden
order of soul-guides, hollow-like *ligaw*, named techniques, a rival from the other side), the teleserye (secrets at a
wake, a sibling given away, a rich family with a hidden link, a reveal at every act break, *"Abangan..."*), and
*Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* (grief, a death date everyone can see, an ensemble whose combat mechanics are their
characters).

**Setting:** Metro Manila, today. Quiapo first: the church, the candle and herb stalls, the anting-anting vendors,
the funeral parlors of Hidalgo Street, the esteros, the Pasig. Folklore is real and modern: spirits live in the
drainage creeks and under the overpasses, and an energy company has learned to bottle them.

## World rules (keep these consistent)

1. **Sundô.** A handful of old families, one per district (the Dimaculangans of Quiapo, the Salongas of Tondo, and
   others to meet later), who see the dead and send them home. A send-off is a *hatid*: say the dead person's whole
   name and that their fare is paid. Magwayen's boat comes for them. She never gets off.
2. **The ninth night.** The dead linger until the *pasiyam*. If they stay too long, or die where no one can send
   them, they forget who they were and become **ligaw**: lost and hungry. Ligaw are what sundô fight. A sundô does
   not hurt the dead; they cut out the hunger that is eating them.
3. **The watch (and its kin).** Every sundô family has a tally cut from Sidapa's balete: the Dimaculangans a pocket
   watch, the Salongas a string of balete beads. It borrows time from Sidapa's tree. It does three things:
   - **Pointed at a person, it shows the time they will die.** That is the season's clock: Teo's own date is the night
     of the eclipse.
   - **Run backward, it borrows time:** a second (undo a blow), a minute, a flood (Lolo Pido's 4 minutes 17 seconds).
   - **It writes everything down.** Borrowed time is paid back by the bearer's family in years. Lolo Pido paid ten
     years for 4:17. Teo's hair greys as he borrows (the game shows it). Borrow until there is nothing left and you
     die old and sudden, like Mang Ambo Salonga.
   - It ticks only for its current bearer. Its powers grow each episode (Rewind, Slow, Echo, the Seventeenth Minute).
4. **Borrowed time has to come from somewhere.** When Lolo pushed the 1998 flood out of Quiapo, the water went
   downriver and drowned thirty-one people in Santa Ana. The Dimaculangans' miracle was someone else's tragedy. This
   is the two-sided cost of the old pact, made personal.
5. **The Kasunduan ng Lupa.** The old pact between the sundô families, the spirit courts (engkanto) and the moon. The
   sundô keep the dead moving; the courts stay on their side; the moon goddess **Mayari** gave up her right to rule
   the moon so the courts could stay neutral. Named in Episode 1, never explained there.
6. **Ginhawa.** Breath, life-force. Hiraya Energy has learned to bottle unsent souls (the ligaw of the esteros) and
   burn them as clean power. Their free-electricity program runs on it.
7. **Bakunawa** is not evil. It is the collector. When the world's borrowed time comes due (the sundô families'
   ledgers, Hiraya's stolen ginhawa, the pact itself), it rises to swallow the moon. The eclipse (*Laho*) is
   collection day.

**Anti-trope guardrails (unchanged):** no chosen-one prophecy (the watch is a family debt, not a destiny; avoid
"chosen", "heir", "destined"), no amnesia, no dead-parents revenge (Teo's mother Marites is an OFW in Dubai whose
remittances never quite cover the interest; his father is out of the picture), no pure-evil corporation, no love triangle, no
mentor death as motivation, no MacGuffin fetch-quest chain. Every character's want conflicts with someone else's.
*Utang na loob* is real pressure. Specific Quiapo detail. Slow burn: **explain the setting, never overstate the story.**

## Cast: want / cost / clash

### Teo Dimaculangan (protagonist, 20)
Night shift at the family parlor, counts chairs and cups because counting is easier than feeling. Has seen the dead
since he was four and has spent fourteen years pretending not to. Fights with a balisong coated in Lola's blessed oil;
inherits Lolo's sundang later in the season.
- **Wants:** to keep the parlor and keep his head down. A normal, unhaunted life with the bills paid.
- **Cost:** his time, literally. Every rewind greys him. And his innocence about his family: Lolo saved Quiapo by
  drowning Santa Ana.
- **Clashes with:** Lola Ising (twenty-eight years of silence), Kardo (keep the proof vs. free the dead), Celeste (the
  sister who got the life he didn't, and the debt he did), Villacorta (who is right about the flood).

### Lola Ising (75)
Runs the parlor; small, unflappable, a cardigan over a housedress. Kept the watch from Pido's death in 2009 without using it.
Planned to pass it to the Salongas and lead Hiraya away with a one-seat bus ticket to Vigan. Taken at 2:40 AM in
Episode 1; held in the Hiraya Wellness Center in Taguig.
- **Wants:** to cut the family loose from the ledger, so Teo never has to pay.
- **Cost:** Teo's trust, and her own daughter's: she gave Marites's baby away in 1998, let Marites believe the flood took her, and has lit a candle for Liwayway every night since.
- **Clashes with:** Teo (her silence), Kardo (she made him keep her secrets), the spirit courts (her "freedom" breaks the contract).

### Kardo Bagwis (53)
In 1998, the young lineman who cut the power and counted Lolo's 257 seconds aloud. Pido asked him to stay near the
family, so he learned to embalm. Drives an old electric-company bucket truck. Party member from Episode 2 (guest in
Episode 1): his mechanic is the **circuit** (Wire, Discharge).
- **Wants:** free power for the poor that doesn't come from Hiraya or from the old pact. He distrusts any deal that
  asks permission from beings who never kept promises to Tondo or Quiapo.
- **Cost:** his certainty. In Episode 3 he learns that tapping the eclipse would burn the same souls Hiraya burns.
- **Clashes with:** Teo (proof vs. mercy: the vial), Sol (archive the past vs. survive the present), Lola Ising.

### Celeste Villacorta, born Liwayway Dimaculangan (28)
Teo's older sister. Hiraya Energy's COO and Gregorio Villacorta's adopted daughter. Has a white streak at her left temple, exactly where
Teo's is. Rival from Episode 1's last frame; joins as a guest, then turns. She uses Hiraya tech: borrowed power with no
personal cost, because someone else pays it.
- **Wants:** to finish her father's work: no family at the mercy of things they cannot see.
- **Cost:** learning what the power is made of, and who she was before she was a Villacorta.
- **Clashes with:** Teo (blood and ideology), her father (in canon, she tells him "You are not saving this country.
  You are selling it."), Lola Ising (the grandmother who gave her away), Marites (the mother who was told she drowned).

### Gregorio Villacorta (45) — Hiraya Energy
At 17, he held his father's hand on a roof in Santa Ana until he couldn't. Built Hiraya to make sure no Filipino family
is ever again at the mercy of what they can't see. The LIWANAG program promises free power to every household in
Metro Manila from the night of the eclipse. **Sympathetic:** he is right about the flood, right about the old pact's
cost, and wrong about the answer.
- **Wants:** energy independence, and an end to the "superstitions" that killed his father.
- **Cost:** the souls he burns, and his daughter.
- **Clashes with:** the sundô families, the courts, Mayari, Celeste.

### Supporting cast
- **Atty. Lorna Benitez** (Hiraya Legal): soft-voiced negotiator. Her pitch is good enough to hurt.
- **Kapitan Obet:** barangay captain and 5-6 lender. Sold the parlor's paper to Hiraya and signed the estero pump
  permit. Petty, ashamed, useful.
- **Mang Domeng:** candle vendor outside Quiapo Church, at every wake on Hidalgo Street for the coffee.
- **Aling Nena, Jun-jun:** Mang Berto's widow and son. Episode 1's case; recurring faces.
- **Aling Tess:** her son Bong went into the estero. Recurring.
- **Mang Ambo Salonga:** the Tondo sundô, found dead in Episode 1, borrowed to nothing. The Salongas are Episode 2's
  thread.
- **Sol Agbayani** (Episode 2+): junior conservator at the National Museum. Can read what is carved in the watch's
  heartwood. Party mechanic: Baybayin glyphs (write a syllable each turn, finished words cast).
- **Mayari** (Episode 7+): the moon goddess who gave up her throne. Mechanic: moon phases (build from crescent to
  full, then release). Her trial tests a moral choice, not strength.
- **Sidapa, Magwayen, Bakunawa:** gods, under their mythological names.

### Episode 1: *Ang Lumang Orasan* (built)
- **Cold open, 3 June 1998:** play as Lolo Pido against the drowned in the flooded parlor (parry tutorial). The
  4:17 rewind. Kardo counts. Pido ages ten years. Downriver, a boy named Villacorta screams for his father. At dawn,
  a knock at the door.
- **Act 1, "Lamay":** night shift at Mang Berto's wake. Kapitan Obet and the debt. The wake hub (Aling Nena, the
  coffin, Mang Domeng, the tong-its table). Berto's ghost. Lola gives Teo the watch (catch QTE). Berto turns ligaw
  (oil QTE, first fight, learn to dodge). The first hatid. Lola answers one question (big choice 1). **Hook:** the
  watch, pointed at Lola, shows 2:40, tonight.
- **Act 2, "Ang Sundô":** salt or an open door. The estero and its hub (Aling Tess, Tanod Celso, the Hiraya guard).
  The creek's unsent rise (fight). Mang Ambo Salonga dead, borrowed to nothing: "DIMACULANGAN. KINOKOLEKTA NA." The
  tagged aswang; the watch awakens and Teo gets his first white streak. Kardo rams the gate (brace QTE). Big choice 2:
  Lola or the van. **Hook:** 2:41. Lola is gone, and her time is gone from the dial.
- **Act 3, "Utang":** Kardo's morgue. The truth about Santa Ana. Lola's tape. Hiraya's offer (big choice 3). The hound,
  fought beside Kardo. The soul in the vial: free it or keep it as proof. Dawn (5:40): Kapitan Obet's betrayal; he was collecting a debt he had already sold. Upstairs, the
  photograph of Liwayway. **Cliffhanger** (6:17 AM; upstairs the clocks still say 4:17): the watch, pointed at Teo, shows the
  eclipse, 47 days away. On TV, a replay of last night's press conference: Celeste Villacorta has his white streak. *"...Liwayway."*

## Endings

The finale reads three things: **utang** (how much time Teo borrowed: his hair), **bonds** (who stands with him), and
**Loob** from the big choices across the season.

1. **Ang Bagong Pacto (The New Covenant): canon / true ending.** Teo does not slay Bakunawa. He offers the family's
   ledger to Sidapa's tree and redraws the Kasunduan so the debt is shared, not inherited: the sundô families, the
   courts, and Hiraya's stolen ginhawa all paid back. Needs strong bonds (Kardo, Sol, Celeste, Mayari) and borrowed
   time kept below the last stage. *Epilogue:* the parlor reopens on Hidalgo Street as a place where the living and
   the dead settle their accounts. Celeste runs a Hiraya that buys sunlight, not souls. Kardo's barangay grid runs on
   honest power. Lola winds the clocks. They read the right time.
2. **Ang Nalagot na Kasunduan (The Severed Veil).** High Loob, weak bonds, Kardo's choices favoured. Teo cuts the
   pact entirely. The spirits vanish from the archipelago; Hiraya collapses; Manila is ordinary and colourless. The
   watch stops for good.
3. **Ang Gabi ng Laho (The Moonless Tide): tragic.** Borrowed past the last stage, or failed at the end. The collector
   takes what it is owed. Bakunawa swallows the moon. Teo, white-haired at twenty, leads the survivors through a
   flooded, moonless Manila.
